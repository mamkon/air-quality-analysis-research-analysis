Research Background and Goals

Can weather patterns help explain ozone levels?

This research examines how temperature, wind speed and solar radiation relate to ground-level ozone. It explores whether these relationships can support estimates of afternoon ozone and help explain differences between locations.

Aim

Understand weather–ozone relationships and assess the usefulness and limitations of regression for estimating afternoon ozone in selected Ontario locations.

Objectives

  • Explore patterns and correlations.
  • Explain data preparation and missing values.
  • Compare regression approaches.
  • Evaluate predictions on later days.
  • Communicate findings and limitations clearly.

The analysis uses correlation and multiple linear regression. Historical New York data from 1973 demonstrate the method separately; recent Ontario data support location-specific models. The historical model is never used to score modern Ontario conditions.

Weather and modelled ozone reference data come from Open-Meteo/CAMS. Official local AQHI context comes from Environment and Climate Change Canada. CAMS reference ozone and AQHI are different measures and are presented separately.

People Behind the Data

Who could use this research?

Public Health and Healthcare Planning

Analysts exploring environmental patterns and questions for future research. Links to hospital visits would require separate health data and validation.

Environmental and Municipal Teams

Researchers, monitoring teams and consultants comparing locations, reviewing model limitations and identifying further evidence needs.

Residents, schools and outdoor employers are wider stakeholders in air-quality information. Decisions about health and outdoor activity should follow official AQHI and public-health advice.
